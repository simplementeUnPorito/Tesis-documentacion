\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

The reported measurements show that embedded mixed-signal resources can recenter a complete low-cost conditioning chain without altering its acquisition path. From an instrumentation perspective, the proposed DA-AFE differs from conventional precision analog front ends, which rely on high-performance components to approach the sensor noise limit \cite{Kirchhoff2017}, and from purely digital offset compensation, which corrects the acquired signal numerically. Instead, the proposed approach uses embedded resources to physically recenter observable additive DC errors within the available VDAC authority and range before acquisition. The reported improvements therefore characterize the complete DA-AFE relative to a common fixed-reference baseline, using a single controller configuration across all evaluated board and gain combinations rather than per-stage parameter optimization.

The foreground strategy also differs from continuous DC-servo architectures, which introduce a settling-versus-bandwidth tradeoff through permanently closed feedback loops \cite{Liu2020,Antoniadis2025}. Here, digital assistance is active only during calibration. Once calibration is completed, the controller is disabled, the VDAC outputs remain constant, and acquisition proceeds through the conventional analog signal path without continuous digital feedback.

\label{sec:pga-note}
The present implementation should be interpreted as a proof of feasibility rather than as an optimized analog front end. The relatively modest PGA improvement illustrates this point. Because $|K_{\mathrm{PGA}}|=1$, the selected deadband ($\Delta_{\mathrm{PGA}}=18$) accepts residuals of approximately $\pm288$~mV (continuous edge near $\pm296$~mV after rounding). Consequently, the 0.24-V residual shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration_trace} reflects the selected stopping criterion rather than controller instability. A separate diagnostic experiment on Board~B at $\times4$ gain reduced the LP residual from a worst-case 2.98\%FS before calibration to a best-case 0.84\%FS with a narrower deadband, indicating that further improvement is possible through parameter optimization. Likewise, the only increase observed at the reported precision (Board~A, SUM stage, $\times50$ gain, from 0.4 to 0.8\%FS) results from deadband acceptance and does not propagate to the downstream LP output.

The timing results further support the practical feasibility of foreground calibration. Visible correction in Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration_trace} spans approximately 7~s, while informal bench observations indicated completion in about 10~s. The configured timeout windows provide a conservative upper bound, and no timeout occurred during the reported experiments. When a stored calibration remains valid, only the per-stage settling intervals are required. The only timeout observed during bench testing occurred while the geophone was moving, violating the quasi-static assumption required for foreground calibration.

The experimental validation was intentionally limited to two independently assembled boards and repeated measurements under eight board--gain configurations. Consequently, the reported statistical results demonstrate repeatability under the evaluated conditions but do not establish device-to-device variability or population-level performance. Long-term drift, environmental effects, inter-channel matching across an array, and optimal controller tuning also remain to be characterized. Furthermore, the present results concern the operating point of the conditioning chain rather than end-to-end seismic performance. Nevertheless, a fixed-reference offset such as the 21.8\%FS measured at the SUM output already consumes more than one fifth of the available amplifier and ADC input range before any seismic signal is applied.

Overall, the proposed DA-AFE provides a practical means of improving the operating conditions of low-cost configurable analog front ends by exploiting embedded mixed-signal resources without modifying the acquisition signal path during normal operation. Foreground DC-offset calibration consistently reduced the LP residual offset across all evaluated board and gain configurations using a single controller configuration. Although demonstrated here through DC-offset calibration, the DA-AFE concept can be extended to other digitally assisted calibration and optimization tasks.
